THE CONNECTION IS MECHANICAL, TOO

Does it plug in, or need solder?

A male end is a metal pin. A female end is a small socket. Match the ends before you connect anything.

Soldered pins or bare holes?

On a board with soldered male headers, top-side circles are solder joints and pins protrude underneath. Female wire sockets plug onto those pins. Bare holes need soldered headers first.

FOR A BOARD WITH BARE HOLES

Solder the header once.
Then plug the wires onto it.

You melt solder, a metal that joins the header pin to the copper pad. You do not melt the board or plastic connector.

A loose pin sitting in a bare hole is not a reliable electrical connection.

  1. Unplug USB and any battery. Use a 2.54 mm male header that fits the board (two 15-pin strips for this 30-pin example).
  2. Put the short ends through the holes. Keep the header square. Long pins must remain exposed for the female jumper sockets.
  3. With supervision if you are new, heat the metal pin and copper pad together. Feed a little solder into the joint, then remove it and the iron.
  4. Let it cool. Check that each joint bonds pin to pad and that neighbouring pads are not bridged. Do not force a connector onto an unsoldered pin.
Potentiometer with flat solder tabs? The breadboard circuit uses a 10 kΩ potentiometer with straight leads that fit separate rows. Flat lugs need suitable soldered leads or an adapter; ordinary female jumper sockets may not fit them. A 3-pin module can have a different pin order: follow its GND / OUT / VCC labels.

Breadboard lead positions: potentiometer at a5 / a7 / a9; resistor at b18 / b23; LED long leg at a23, short leg at a25. Use these positions only if your component spacing fits—never force the legs.

Follow the printed labels. A0 reads the knob. D5 is GPIO14 and drives the LED through a resistor. 3V supplies 3.3 V; G is ground. This potentiometer arrangement requires the board’s A0 to accept 0–3.3 V.

Wiring alone will not vary the LED brightness. The ESP8266 must run a matching program that reads A0 and produces PWM on D5 (GPIO14).

PIN LABELS, NOT PIN NUMBERS

Find a board pin

USB at the bottom, antenna at the top. Select a label to inspect it. D5 means GPIO14, not GPIO5 or the fifth physical pin. Repeated G labels are ground; the guide names which G to use.

INSIDE THE BREADBOARD

Five holes share one connection.

Holes a23, b23, c23, d23 and e23 touch the same internal metal strip. The LED’s long leg at a23 touches the resistor’s end at b23 through that strip.

Row 24 is separate from row 23. The middle gap also separates a–e from f–j. The coloured side rails have their own strips and can be split; this example does not use them.
